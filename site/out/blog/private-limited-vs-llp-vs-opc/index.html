Private Limited vs LLP vs OPC: which one should you register?

A plain-English comparison of Private Limited Company, LLP and One Person Company: owners, compliance, tax, funding and cost, so you can pick the right structure.

Most founders in India choose between three structures: a Private Limited Company, a Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) or a One Person Company (OPC). All three give you limited liability, which means your personal assets are protected if the business runs into debt. The differences are in who can own it, how much compliance it needs and how investors see it.

Private Limited Company

Needs at least two directors and two shareholders (they can be the same people). There is no minimum capital. It is the only structure that can issue shares to angel investors and venture capital funds, and it is the structure most banks and large clients trust.

  • Best for: startups planning to raise funds, hire employees with stock options or scale fast.
  • Compliance: board meetings, statutory audit every year, annual ROC filings (AOC-4 and MGT-7) and income tax return.

LLP

Needs at least two designated partners. Ownership is through the LLP agreement rather than shares. Compliance is lighter: an audit is needed only above a turnover or contribution limit, and the annual filings are Form 11 and Form 8.

  • Best for: professional firms, consultancies and family businesses that do not plan to raise equity.
  • Limitation: investors usually cannot buy equity in an LLP.

One Person Company

A company with a single owner, who must be an Indian citizen resident in India, plus a nominee. It has the credibility of a company with only one founder. It can be converted into a Private Limited Company later as the business grows.

How to decide

If you plan to raise money or give ESOPs, choose a Private Limited Company. If you are two or more partners running a services business with no funding plans, an LLP keeps costs down. If you are a solo founder who wants a company from day one, start with an OPC.
